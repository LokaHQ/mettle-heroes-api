# Reply drafting

> Read [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md) for the house rules, tone and known out-of-date FAQ answers, and [CONNECTORS.md](../../CONNECTORS.md) if you see a `~~` placeholder.

## 1. Understand the ticket

Read the ticket or message you were given. Note the client, their plan, the category, and what they want: an answer, a fix, a refund, an apology or a date.

## 2. Find the answer

- Look it up in the FAQ: the `~~knowledge base` page if connected, otherwise the FAQ file you were given.
- Check the answer against CONTEXT.md. If they disagree, CONTEXT.md is right. Use the correct answer in the reply and list the FAQ answer as out of date.
- If neither has the answer, don't invent one. Write the reply so it says who will confirm and by when, and flag it for the team lead.

## 3. Write the draft

- Follow the tone in CONTEXT.md: plain, warm, short sentences, one apology, then what happens next and when.
- Use the client's name and the ticket or booking reference.
- Keep it under 150 words unless the client asked several questions.

## 4. Hand it back

Give the draft, then a short note under it:

- **Sources:** which FAQ answer or house rule you used.
- **Out-of-date FAQ answers:** any you found, with the correct answer. Say "none" if there were none.
- **Check before sending:** anything a person must confirm, such as a refund amount or a date.

Never send the reply yourself. A person on the team sends it.
